Banana Bread Recipe for Kids: A Simple Weekend Bake

15 Oct 2026Master ArtistBakingParents

Banana bread is one of the easiest things to bake with children: one bowl, no mixer, no kneading, and very ripe bananas do most of the work. Children aged around four and up can mash, pour and stir, while an adult handles the oven. Mix it in 15 minutes, bake it for about an hour, and you have a loaf the whole family will eat.

It is also one of the recipes we bake at our Sunday baking workshops in Desa Sri Hartamas, because it works just as well for a five-year-old as it does for a grandparent. Here is a simple version to try at home this weekend.

Why banana bread is a good first bake for kids

Plenty of recipes look simple until a child is in charge of the whisk. Banana bread is genuinely forgiving, for a few reasons:

  • The batter wants to be lumpy. Over-mixing is the most common mistake, so a child who stops stirring early is doing exactly the right thing.
  • Soft, spotty bananas mash easily. A fork and a bowl are enough. There is no chopping and no sharp knife involved.
  • It rescues food. Those brown bananas nobody wanted become the best part of the recipe, which is a nice lesson in not wasting food.
  • Every job is visible. Children can see the mash, the batter and the rise in the oven, so they understand what each step is for.

Baking also brings in some quiet learning along the way: counting spoonfuls, reading a recipe in order, and waiting patiently while something bakes.

Ingredients for one loaf

This makes one standard loaf (roughly a 20 x 10cm tin). Measuring by weight is the most reliable way, and kitchen scales are a good job for an older child.

  • 3 very ripe bananas (about 300g once peeled) — the more black spots, the better
  • 75g butter, melted and cooled slightly (or 70ml neutral cooking oil)
  • 100g brown sugar (you can reduce this to 80g if your bananas are very sweet)
  • 1 egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 190g plain flour
  • 1 teaspoon bicarbonate of soda
  • A pinch of salt
  • Optional extras: a handful of raisins, chocolate chips or chopped walnuts, and flaked almonds for the top

Most local bananas work well, as long as they are properly ripe. If yours are still yellow, leave them in a paper bag for a day or two. You can also freeze ripe bananas whole and thaw them when you are ready to bake.

Step by step: who does what

Before you start, heat the oven to 175°C (or 160°C fan) and line or grease the loaf tin. Everyone washes their hands, and long hair gets tied back.

  1. Mash the bananas (child). Peel them into a large bowl and mash with a fork until mostly smooth. A few small lumps are fine.
  2. Add the wet ingredients (child, with help). Pour in the melted butter, then the sugar, egg and vanilla. Cracking the egg into a separate cup first makes it easy to fish out any shell.
  3. Stir (child). Mix with a wooden spoon until it looks evenly combined.
  4. Add the dry ingredients (child). Tip in the flour, bicarbonate of soda and salt. Fold gently with a spatula only until no dry flour is showing. Stop there — lumpy is right.
  5. Add the extras (child). Stir in raisins or chocolate chips if you are using them.
  6. Fill the tin (child, with help). Scrape the batter into the tin and smooth the top. Children love scattering flaked almonds or a few banana slices over the top.
  7. Bake (adult). Bake for 50–60 minutes. Ovens vary, so check from 50 minutes: a skewer pushed into the middle should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. If the top browns too quickly, lay a sheet of foil loosely over it.
  8. Cool (everyone, patiently). Leave it in the tin for 10 minutes, then lift it out onto a rack. It slices much more neatly once it has cooled.

If you would like a tested recipe written specifically for young cooks, America's Test Kitchen Kids has a banana bread recipe with clear, child-friendly steps.

Matching jobs to your child's age

Every child is different, so treat these as a rough guide rather than rules. Children enjoy baking far more when they have a real job, not just a turn at stirring.

AgeGood jobs in this recipe
4–5Peeling and mashing bananas, tipping in pre-measured ingredients, scattering toppings
6–8Cracking the egg, measuring spoonfuls, folding the batter, greasing the tin
9–12Weighing ingredients on scales, reading the recipe aloud, checking doneness with a skewer (with an adult at the oven)

Whatever their age, an adult should stay in the kitchen and handle the hot oven and tins. Kansas State University's guide to cooking with kids at every age is a useful reference if you want more ideas for age-appropriate kitchen tasks.

Easy variations to try next time

Once your family has made the basic loaf, let your child choose a twist. Making a small change and comparing the results is a good way to build confidence in the kitchen.

  • Muffins: spoon the batter into a lined muffin tray and bake for around 18–22 minutes. Smaller bakes are great for younger children, who find waiting an hour hard.
  • Chocolate chip: fold in a handful of chocolate chips, keeping a few back to press on top.
  • Nutty and fruity: add chopped walnuts and raisins, with flaked almonds on top.
  • Less sugar: use the ripest bananas you can find and reduce the sugar a little. The bananas bring plenty of natural sweetness.

Wrapped well, banana bread keeps for two to three days at room temperature, and slices freeze well for school snack boxes.

Frequently asked questions

What age can children start baking banana bread?

Children as young as four can help by mashing bananas and pouring in pre-measured ingredients. Older children can take on weighing, folding and reading the recipe, while an adult always handles the oven.

Why is my banana bread dense or rubbery?

The usual cause is over-mixing once the flour goes in. Fold gently and stop as soon as no dry flour is visible. Under-ripe bananas can also make the loaf drier and less sweet.

Can I make banana bread without eggs?

Yes. Many egg-free versions replace the egg with an extra half banana or a few tablespoons of plain yoghurt. The loaf may be slightly denser, but it is still delicious.
