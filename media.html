01 · Photography

From camera to final edit

Taught by Alden, founder of WaffleBox Productions, with years of experience shooting graduations, events, food and products. Students learn how the camera works, then how to see — light, framing, people and moments — and finish with work they're proud to show.

How each month works

  1. Brief & conceptThe month's idea is demonstrated and the assignment set.
  2. ShootStudents photograph, with individual guidance.
  3. Cull & editChoosing which frames survive — a skill taught in its own right — then a group review.
  4. Reshoot & selectShoot again with corrections in mind, then pick the best for their folder.

Join at the start of any month and stay as long as you like. Every assignment comes in three levels — new, returning and experienced — so beginners and long-standing students work on the same theme side by side. Each student builds a folder of their best work as they go.

The seven monthly themes

Month 1

The Camera

How the tool works: lenses against human vision, camera types (with a Polaroid developing on the table), handling, cards and why RAW matters.

Assignment: Photograph one person at three focal lengths — and watch the background change completely.

Month 2

Light

Hard and soft light, direction, and exposure — aperture, shutter speed and ISO as three ways of controlling light.

Assignment: Make one ordinary object look like morning, then midday, then a horror film.

Month 3

The Frame

Composition as decisions, not rules: distance, angle, and what to leave out.

Assignment: Make one cheap object look expensive, then lonely, then menacing.

Month 4

People

Portraits — putting a subject at ease, shooting from the right height, and one-light setups. Students photograph each other.

Assignment: Shoot your partner as the hero of a film, then as the villain.

Month 5

Things on Tables

Food and product photography: surfaces, angles and lighting a small subject.

Assignment: Photograph a plate of RM8 food so it looks like it cost RM50.

Month 6

Moment

Shutter speed and anticipation — photographing things that won't wait.

Assignment: Catch something at the exact instant it can only be caught once.

Month 7

The Edit

Culling at volume, then editing: exposure, colour, crop, and knowing when to stop.

Assignment: Cut your best hundred frames down to ten — then defend the ten.

Equipment

  • Have a camera? Bring it — any brand works
  • Concepts are taught on the whiteboard, not one camera’s menus
  • Bring your camera manual, or have it on your phone
  • Lighting for class work is provided

Is this right for you?

  • Ages 12 and up, including adults
  • Complete beginners — or phone photographers ready for more
  • Anyone who wants to make pictures, not just take them

02 · Videography & Film

Shoot, edit, tell a story

Taught by Zimin, who brings a filmmaker's eye to every project — from concept to final cut. Students shoot, edit and finish a real film, learning to turn raw footage into stories worth watching.

What you'll learn

  • Planning a story and a shot list
  • Shooting with real equipment
  • Editing raw footage into a finished film
  • Finding your own visual voice

Is this right for your child?

  • Ages 12 and up, including adults
  • Kids who already film on their phones and want to go further
  • Anyone who loves films and wants to make one
